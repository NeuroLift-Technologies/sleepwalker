Two flashpoints · two arcs

It already happened. Twice — and both times, it bent back.

Each upgrade followed the same shape: something people loved, changed without consent, met with backlash — then a partial walk-back. The walk-back is the point. It proves the fix is possible when a vendor chooses it.

The ask · reasonable, not reactionary

Three things we’re asking vendors for

We’re not anti-upgrade. Each of these has already been proven to work — by users, in public, during the two flashpoints above.

01

Warn us

Announce a behavioral or personality change before it ships — not after people notice their assistant feels like a stranger.

Proven: nobody got a heads-up either time — and the silence is exactly what people were angriest about.

02

Let us keep the old one

Offer a supported path to stay on the model a person depends on, for as long as it’s reasonable to maintain.

Proven: GPT-4o was restored as an option after sustained outcry. The capability exists.

03

Make it opt-in

New behavior should be a choice, with a way back — defaults that respect the relationship already in progress.

Proven: Opus effort controls already let power-users dial the new behavior in. Make that the front door.
